The Deregulation Campaign.

A working series on what happens when regulation changes the price and organization of labour, firms respond with automation, and the resulting systems reshape how work, ownership and attention move.

This campaign begins from the shop floor rather than the abstraction. Deregulation changes incentives. Firms reorganize. Automation becomes economically attractive in some tasks while skilled physical work becomes more valuable in others. The web then turns older attention mechanisms into measurable infrastructure.

The opening public note is deliberately readable without registration. The deeper papers, source notes and offshoots are the reader relationship: enter an email address once to receive the full campaign and future releases.

Offshoot 01: The balloon was already an algorithm.

A branded hot-air balloon makes the attention problem visible without a platform. Contrast captures the eye; novelty creates memory; a photograph turns the physical impression into a digital artifact; conversation propagates it. The web did not invent those primitives. It instrumented them.
