39 DAYS

A Not Your Fucking Tea Original

I. THE STORAGE UNIT

People want the lightning bolt. They want the montage — the single dramatic morning where the music swells and the old life falls off your shoulders like a coat you finally decided not to wear.

Nobody tells you it actually looks like a storage unit.

The unit became the gym.

And the boxes didn't move. I did.

QL = (WN × SL)

Quality of Life equals Wins Noticed times Standard of Life.

II. THE DOLLAR EIGHTY

Small story first, because the small ones are where you can actually see yourself.

Refusing to tap the card is a rep. Same muscle. Different room.

III. THE SEQUENCE

Here's the sequence, plainly, because plain sequence is the sharpest instrument there is.

On the fourth, I put it on the record. A doctor's note.

By the fifth, the paper had already been written. Not delivered. Written.

And on the morning of the seventh, they handed me a letter dated two days earlier, telling me I wasn't the right fit.

Fit isn't dated. Reactions are.

IV. SAME BODY, DIFFERENT HAND

They think the needle is the story.

It never was.

Dysregulated, I don't just fail to notice. I fail to retrieve.

What actually worked was standing up.

Wins Noticed isn't a gratitude practice. It's a retrieval protocol.

Turns out the body starts anyway. Slower. Nothing dramatic. It just gets up, because getting up is a decision now instead of a chemical event.

Nothing isn't available to a living body. There is always a rep the body can produce without a catalyst.

One hundred and nine.

V. THE FILE

I had the answer to their question in my pocket the entire time.

One arm of the government certified the duration. Another arm denied support on the grounds that the duration wasn't established.

That's not inspiration. That's mechanics.

VI. THE SIGNAL

There's a habit I've had since I was a kid that I never had a name for until this year, and it turns out it's not a party trick. It's data.

I write down what I notice before I know why I noticed it.

Two entries, close together, both worth putting on the record. One: a dream about being let go from a job, logged on the second of August. The actual termination landed on the seventh. Five days. Two: a passing thought about a tool going missing, also on the second. A drill went missing at work on the third. One day.

I want to be precise about what I'm claiming, because it would be easy to overclaim and I'd rather be right than interesting. I'm not saying the writing caused the outcome. I'm not building a prophecy.

I don't know yet what the gap means. I know it's there, and I know ignoring it would be the old version of me, the one that couldn't open his own drawer.

VII. BORING MYTHIC

I've decided true is more useful to me than heroic from here forward.

That's the boring mythic. Slow-clapping for yourself in an empty room because you did the unremarkable thing on time again.

VIII. WHO'S AUTHORING THIS

Who is offering this reality?

I'm done outsourcing the pen.

My sister called this year, first real conversation we'd had since May, and near the bottom of it she told me something I needed to hear and didn't want to: that what I was going through was affecting everyone around me, not just me.

The friction doesn't stay in the room where it started. It moves.

IX. THE VERDICT

On the fourth, I handed them a doctor's note.

On the fifth, they wrote my termination letter.

They didn't need twenty-four hours to decide I wasn't a fit. They needed twenty-four hours to decide what to call it.

They can't answer that either way without answering it.

I didn't get fired. I got handed a confession — dated, signed, and timestamped by people who assumed nobody in my position reads that closely.

Some sequences don't survive being looked at.

This one didn't survive discovery.

X. DAY 39

I don't know exactly what day thirty-nine looks like from here. That's not modesty. That's the entire architecture.

But the reps aren't there to earn the noticing. The reps are there to make the noticing possible. I don't move because I've figured it out. I move so that I can.

One hundred push.

One hundred pull.

One hundred squats.

And a hundred and nine sit-ups.

That's not penance. That's interest.

I didn't get clean. I got even.

Thirty-nine days. Same hell, different levels. I'll see you on the other side of it — with the paperwork, and the proof, and the pen still in my hand.

— Savant
