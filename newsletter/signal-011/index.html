NYF Signal 011East Corner / cross-source synthesis

The response architecture is moving. The exposure is multiplying.

Regional institutions are formalizing climate and humanitarian response while constitutional rupture, cross-border violence and Ebola expose how quickly protection requirements are expanding.

11 November 2026 · 04:00 MST6 source recordsReviewed synthesis

Two institutions moved from aspiration toward operating architecture. Uganda and the African Union signed the host agreement for a continental humanitarian agency with a stated financing commitment, while the EAC adopted an El Niño plan with named structures and November implementation deadlines.

The exposure is multiplying faster. Somalia’s constitutional dispute now carries a Supreme Court timetable, a suspected al-Shabaab attack killed eight people at a Kenyan road project, and Congo’s Ebola outbreak has crossed 11,000 confirmed cases. Kenya’s release of its last patient shows that containment is possible, but only provisionally.

Signal 011 reads these records together because response capacity should be measured against the speed, scale and geography of the shock—not the announcement of an institution alone.

Source record

Evidence behind the synthesis
African Union9 Nov 2026

African Union and Uganda sign the host agreement for the African Humanitarian Agency.

The African Union and Uganda signed the host agreement establishing the African Humanitarian Agency in Kampala. Uganda committed US$86 million in three tranches over three years; the AU said interim staff are expected to move by mid-December and five regional depots are planned.

Why it matters: The agreement supplies a legal base, a host state and a stated financing commitment for a continent-led response institution. The unresolved point is whether the tranches, staffing and depot network arrive on the announced timetable.

East African Community9 Nov 2026

EAC adopts a regional El Niño emergency plan with November implementation deadlines.

The EAC adopted a joint plan covering early warning, health, infrastructure, food security and climate displacement. It directed the Secretariat to prepare an implementation plan by November 30 and work with Kenya on a regional disaster-information dashboard by November 25.

Why it matters: The plan converts a regional climate warning into named coordination structures and near-term deadlines. Financing remains to be mobilized, so operational readiness cannot yet be inferred from adoption alone.

Associated Press6 Nov 2026

Somalia’s Supreme Court rules the president’s term has expired.

Somalia’s Supreme Court rejected the claim that constitutional changes extended the incumbent president’s mandate. It gave political leaders 30 days to agree on an election process, ordered elections within 90 days and limited the government to routine administration while a vote is pending.

Why it matters: The ruling creates a judicial timetable for a political dispute already linked to armed tension in Mogadishu. Enforcement, interim authority and the presidency’s response remain unresolved.

Reuters10 Nov 2026

Eight people are killed in a suspected al-Shabaab attack in northeastern Kenya.

Two police reservists, five construction workers and a driver were killed in an ambush near a road project in Mandera County. Police and a regional official said the attackers were suspected al-Shabaab militants who may have crossed from Somalia; investigations were still underway.

Why it matters: The attack shows how insecurity can directly interrupt infrastructure work and civilian mobility along the Kenya–Somalia border. Attribution remains suspected rather than judicially established.

Reuters10 Nov 2026

Congo’s Ebola outbreak surpasses 11,000 confirmed cases.

Congolese government data showed 11,042 confirmed infections and 5,387 deaths across eight provinces. The Bundibugyo outbreak has no approved treatment or vaccine, and delayed detection, conflict and strained health services have accelerated its spread.

Why it matters: The scale makes regional containment capacity—not only national case management—the operative test. The totals are government data and may change as surveillance and delayed reporting continue.

Read source ↗
Reuters11 Nov 2026

Kenya releases its last Ebola patient and starts the 42-day disease-free countdown.

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient after recording nine cases, two deaths and seven recoveries. No new infection has been recorded since October 19, the last 11 contacts left quarantine, and arrivals from Congo will face a 21-day quarantine.

Why it matters: Kenya’s containment progress contrasts with the expanding outbreak in Congo and shows the value of contact tracing around an imported cluster. Disease-free status is conditional on completing 42 days without another case.

Editorial rule

Count the institution, then count the distance to operation.
