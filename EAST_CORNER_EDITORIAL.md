# East Corner editorial contract

East Corner is an editorial desk, not a summarization service.

## The product

East Corner follows enterprise, capital, culture and overlooked builders across the Horn of Africa, East Africa and its diaspora. The source record establishes what happened. East Corner's product is the authored frame: what the desk notices, connects, questions, finds consequential, and chooses to put in front of the reader.

The founding instruction remains: **Look where the centre does not.** We follow receipts, not proximity to power. We start with the material event, locate the lens the usual frame leaves out, connect supporting beats, and leave the reader with a sharper way to see what is happening.

A reader should not be able to replace East Corner with a generic AI summary of the same links. If the copy merely restates source reporting, it is not finished.

## Voice

East Corner is specific, curious, economically literate, culturally alive and willing to make an argument. It does not write like an institutional briefing unless the material itself requires that register. It can notice absurdity, ambition, contradiction, status, beauty, incentives, power, taste, money and ordinary human behaviour in the same edition.

The desk does not flatten East Africa into development language. People are builders, consumers, artists, operators, investors, workers, officials, neighbours and characters in their own right — not symbols in somebody else's regional thesis.

The voice should be recognizable without the masthead. Headlines are propositions, not labels. Openings should create a reason to keep reading. Connections must earn themselves from the evidence rather than being imposed because a taxonomy exists.

## Editorial test

Before publication, the editor must be able to answer four questions:

1. What is East Corner saying that the source material does not already say for us?
2. Why does this belong in our reader's morning rather than merely in our database?
3. What connection, contradiction, character or consequence did we notice?
4. Is every factual claim that needs a receipt traceable to a source?

If the first three answers are weak, more aggregation is not the cure. The piece needs editorial work.

## Sources and infrastructure

Sources are evidence, not voice. Automation may discover, deduplicate, timestamp, normalize, rank and preserve source material. It may prepare the desk. It must not confuse a successful collection run with a finished East Corner edition.

Taxonomy belongs underneath the product for retrieval. It must not force the published edition into artificial lanes. The strongest story determines the shape.

The Morning Wire may expose source-linked discovery as a utility, but it is not a substitute for the authored East Corner edition. A numbered Signal or other principal East Corner publication must contain original editorial framing and synthesis.

## Deadline

**Four a.m. America/Edmonton is a publication deadline, not a build-start time.**

By four a.m., the reader-facing morning product must already be live, readable and checked. Collection and technical preflight happen earlier. Infrastructure failure is not editorial permission to publish late, and editorial work is not permission to ignore deployment reliability.

The operating target is therefore: collect early, create deliberately, verify, deploy, and leave recovery margin before four a.m.

## Division of labour

Infrastructure manages repetition. Editorial talent makes choices.

The system should remove mechanical distance between an idea and publication: source capture, normalized records, routes, canonical metadata, RSS, search discovery, audit and deployment should be fast and boring. The saved time belongs to reporting, thinking, writing, editing and taste.

East Corner succeeds when the architecture disappears behind a publication that feels authored, current and unmistakably its own.
